Week 3. Hand Tools and Fabrication

Kinetic Sculpture Final Project Prep

Given the specs of my final project idea, I thought this week would be a good opportunity to get ahead on some of the motor-driven aspects. I originally thought of building the x axis of the pulley system set on a v slot, with the gantry attached to the belt. You can see the vision in the scratchwork. However, given it was due on Thursday and I walked into the lab on Wednesday, I took Bobby’s advice to pare the scope down a little bit. The key insight would be learning to use a stepper motor and making gears.
  • Thus, I decided to make a carousel roughly inspired by Kassia’s spinning dancer example. However, I didn’t want the intermediary spinning gears to be stationary—I wanted them to revolve and rotate. This meant I would have to create a gear to attach to the stepper motor, spinning spur gears, and an internal gear to house and help drive the motion. Some of my notes in this design process are shown here. I had to download GF Gear generator from the Fusion App Store as well as learn key dimensions of gears—principally, the module and number of teeth, which multiply to pitch height. Tinkering with these quantities was a big part of this assignment.
    • Bobby was extremely helpful with all of this, including finding materials and showing me how to wire. Materials included an A4988 motor driver, the ESP32 microcontroller, a stepper motor, and some wiring with a breadboard (as shown). Using the “A4988 Stepper Motor Driver Carrier” documentation from Pololu was extremely informative. Then, we hooked the motor to my computer, and Bobby showed me the code to drive the stepper motor. It made sense given the Youtube video I had watched on how stepper motors actually work.

const int stepPin = 18;
const int dirPin = 19;

void setup() {
  pinMode(dirPin, OUTPUT);
  pinMode(stepPin, OUTPUT);

void loop() {
  digitalWrite(dirPin,HIGH);
  for (int i =0; i < 10000; i++)
  {
    digitalWrite(stepPin, HIGH);
    delay(1);
    digitalWrite(stepPin, LOW);
    delay(1);
  }
  digitalWrite(dirPin,LOW);
  for (int i =0; i < 10; i++)
  {
    digitalWrite(stepPin, HIGH);

  }

    Once we got that going, it was off to the races w.r.t. gears. started designing them using the tools native to fusion and following Youtube tutorials. That went fine. But then I realized it’s not very easy to create internal gears without the gear generator plugin, so I installed it and remade all the gears using that plugin instead. Setting the sum of the pitch radii of the two spur gears equal to the radius of the internal gear, I laser cut gears out of cardboard. I slotted them onto the stepper motor and then discovered the first obstacle: the material choice. It became clear that using cardboard would not be effective since the internal fluting would impede proper movement.
    When putting the cams together (one smaller circle with two slightly larger circles around it), I realized that it would probably work a bit better to have two smaller circles in the center so that the track that the moving dowels follow would fit the diameter of the dowels better. I only had two smaller circles in my Fusion sketch, so I just made one cam for this test run and decided to print extra in my next test. I used hot glue to put the cam together and to attach the walls to the plank piece (also realized I made two plank pieces but only one hole so I could only use one of them...).
    final mechanism
    A couple of additional bits worth mentioning here. Eventually the gear would go around a full rotation and behave. However, there would be occasional times where it would get a little jammed--nothing a little manual agitation could not fix. Watching carefully, I realized that I needed to add more supports to prevent the platform from tilting. I could have added another gear to the other side as a counterbalance, but I liked the idea of a stable base on its own. I used the drill press, a bit of glue, and scrap wood to build the legs. Second, I added a little wire flower to spin around! This was a bit lazy, but I like it.
    Overall, I was quite pleased with how this came out. There's definitely a laundry list of things that would make it better--for example, building a slot for the stepper motor to sit stably in, reducing the module/increasing the number of teeth to reduce how much the gears get caught, maybe a more elegant figurine on the spur gear, etc. Stay tuned for next week for those updates!.
